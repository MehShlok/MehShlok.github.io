Hi, I'm Shlok.

I'm a final year undergraduate at BITS Pilani, Goa, dual majoring in Mathematics and Computer Science. I'm currently a visiting scholar supervised by Dr. Alan Yuille at Johns Hopkins University, working on continual learning through the lens of mechanistic interpretability for my thesis.

My path here was indirect. I grew up moving across India, picking up varied cultures and languages along the way, and a keen interest in mathematics. In college I built deep learning systems for a Mars rover team, started research in statistical learning theory under Dr. Snehanshu Saha, and submitted my first paper in my second year, under Dr. JK Sahoo.

Research

I'm interested in understanding how neural networks learn: how they extract meaningful representations from data, and why their learning dynamics unfold the way they do. I approach this through deep learning theory and mechanistic interpretability, studying the internal structure that emerges during training and what it reveals about generalisation. More broadly, I'm drawn to problems at the interface of mathematics, machine learning, and the brain.
